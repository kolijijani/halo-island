# Halo Island privacy

Halo Island is made by kolijijani. It needs no account, and has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Unless you sign in to Halo, it never sends us anything about you. On its own it reads one public file from our website, to see whether a new version is out; the Stocks add-on, once you switch it on, also reads public prices from Yahoo Finance.

## What leaves your PC

**Lyrics lookups.** When a song plays and **Synced lyrics** is on, Halo Island asks [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free public lyrics library, for that song's lyrics. The request carries the song's title, artist, album and length, plus a User-Agent that names Halo Island and links to this project. LRCLIB is run by other people and has its own terms. Answers are cached on your PC, so a song isn't looked up again every time it plays.

To stop lookups, right-click the island or the tray icon and turn off **Synced lyrics**.

**Apple Music lyrics, only if you turn them on.** If you add your Apple Music token (right-click the island, then **Apple Music lyrics › Use token from clipboard**), Halo Island also asks Apple Music for lyrics the way Apple's web player does. It checks the token, searches Apple Music's catalog for the playing song's artist and title, and downloads that song's lyrics. These requests go to Apple (`music.apple.com` and `amp-api.music.apple.com`) and carry your token; the token goes nowhere else. To stop, choose **Remove token** in the same menu.

**Phone drop, only while its panel is open.** When you open Phone drop on the Shelf, Halo Island serves a small web page on your local network so your phone can send files to the Shelf and save Shelf files. Only a device on the same network with that session's secret link, which only the QR code carries, can open it. Files go straight between your phone and your PC: nothing passes through the internet or through us. Halo Island reads nothing from an upload until you click **Accept**. The page stops working when you click **Done**, or after 10 minutes without a phone.

**Paired phones, only after you pair one.** **Pair a phone** (Shelf › Phone) shows a QR code with a pairing secret made on your PC. The phone that scans it keeps the secret, and so does your PC, sealed so only your Windows account can read it. From then on, while Halo Island runs, it keeps one request open at our small meeting server, `drop.halo.com`, so the phone can find the PC from any network. The server sees your PC's and phone's IP addresses, when they are online, and an address for the pair that is derived from the secret; it never sees the secret itself, a device name, a file name or a file. Every message through it is signed with the secret, so it can't pretend to be your phone or your PC. Files then go straight between phone and PC, encrypted by WebRTC with keys made on the two devices. When both networks block a direct link, they pass through a relay run by Cloudflare that forwards the encrypted bytes and can't read them. To find its own internet address, Halo Island also asks a public STUN server (Cloudflare's or Google's) when a phone connects. **Remove** a phone in **Paired phones** and the pair stops working on both sides; with no paired phones, nothing is sent at all.

**News, while Today is open.** The Today page reads the top stories of the news source picked in Settings (BBC World by default; CNA, Al Jazeera or Bernama), straight from that publisher's public feed, when the page opens and at most every 15 minutes while it stays open. The request carries nothing about you beyond what any web request does (your IP address, and Halo Island's name and version as its user agent); the publisher's own privacy policy covers it. The stories stay in memory only.

**Halo, only if you sign in.** Signing in (the gear on the open island, then **Sign in with Halo**; or right-click the island, then **Halo › Sign in to Halo…**; on a Mac, also the menu bar under Halo's mark) connects this computer to your [halo.app](https://halo.app) account: you approve a code in halo.app, and Halo Island gets a key of its own. It then asks `api.halo.app` whether your account has Studio Pro, which the Downloader and Meeting notes need (as it signs in or starts with the Downloader on, and when the Add-ons or Notes tab opens), and sends nothing else for that. It sends its key, its version and your PC's UTC offset with each request, and the PC's name once, when you sign in. It asks nothing about your money: no transactions, balances, bills, account numbers, notes or receipts, and it can't change or delete anything or touch your account. Halo Island has no Health page and asks for no steps or other health data. Halo's own privacy page covers what happens there: [halo.com/privasi](https://halo.com/privasi). **Disconnect** in Settings (or **Halo › Disconnect** in the menu) signs this PC out and removes the key and everything Halo Island kept from Halo; you can also disconnect it in halo.app.

**AI usage, only with Studio Pro, while the AI tab is open or its notices are on.** The AI tab shows how much is left of the AI coding plans you use on this computer (Codex, Claude Code and the others in the table below). To fill a card, Halo Island reads that service's own sign-in on your computer (the files in the table, or a key you typed) and asks that service's own server how much of your plan is used. It reads when the tab opens and every minute while the tab stays open, and asks each service's server at most every few minutes ([docs/ai-usage.md](docs/ai-usage.md) has each one's pace). If you switch on **Tell me before I run out**, it also reads every 5 minutes while the tab is closed and asks each server at most every 10 minutes, so the island can warn you before a limit runs out; that stops while the computer is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on. With the tab closed and the notices off, nothing runs.

**Each sign-in goes only to its own service.** A sign-in or key read here is sent only to the server of the service it belongs to (Claude Code's to `api.anthropic.com`, Codex's to `chatgpt.com`, an OpenRouter key to `openrouter.ai`), never to Halo or anyone else, and never into the log. Nothing about your usage goes to Halo: the numbers come from each service straight to your computer and stay in its memory. Each request carries a User-Agent naming Halo Island and its version, never another app's. Halo Island only reads: it never changes another app's files and never renews another app's sign-in, which could sign you out of that app, so when a sign-in has run out, the card asks you to open that app once. On a Mac, a sign-in kept in the keychain is read with macOS's own `security` tool, only while the AI tab is open and at most once each time you open it; if macOS asks you first and you say no, it isn't asked again until you press **Refresh**.

**Keys you type, only for the services that need one.** OpenRouter, Z.ai and MiniMax read your usage with a key you make on their site and type into the AI tab, under **Services**; Kimi Code and OpenCode Go take one too. A key goes only to its own service's servers, as above. Kimi Code, Z.ai and MiniMax each also have a server in mainland China for accounts made there, so a key their other server refuses is tried there too, on each refresh until it works or you remove it. Halo Island keeps a key sealed so only your Windows account on this PC can read it (on a Mac, in a file only your Mac account can read, which Time Machine leaves out of its backups), never in your settings or the log.

**To undo any of it:** hide a service under **Services** and Halo Island stops reading it. **Remove** a key you typed and its sealed copy is deleted; to stop the key working anywhere, delete it on the service's own site too (such as OpenRouter's keys page). Switch off **Tell me before I run out** and nothing reads while the tab is closed.

**Add-ons, only the ones you switch on.** Add-ons (the last tab, a "+") are extra features whose files aren't part of the app. Switching one on (in that tab, or Japanese readings from the offer under a Japanese song's lyrics) downloads its files from this project's releases on GitHub, or, for the Downloader's yt-dlp and QuickJS-ng, from those projects' own releases there (`github.com`, which serves them from `release-assets.githubusercontent.com`), the same way your browser downloads the installer. Nothing about you goes with the request beyond what any download carries (your IP address and a User-Agent naming Halo Island). Each file is checked against the size and fingerprint built into the app and refused if it differs. Switching the add-on off deletes its files. While every add-on is off, nothing is downloaded.

**New versions.** About two minutes after it starts, Halo Island reads one small file, `kolijijani.github.io/halo-island/latest.json`, which names the latest version (never while the PC is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on), and again whenever you pick **Check for updates…** in the menu or **Check now** in Settings. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages. The request carries nothing about you beyond what any page view carries (your IP address, and a User-Agent naming Halo Island and its version), and we get no record of it. The file is signed, and the app ignores it unless the signature checks out. On Windows, once the file names a newer version, the app downloads that version's Setup from this project's releases on GitHub, once, the way add-ons download, and keeps it in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Halo\update` until you install it; it is deleted once you run that version. On a Mac it does the same with the DMG, in `~/Library/Caches/Halo/update`, with the app it takes out of it.

**Prayer times' location, only while that add-on is on.** To pick the nearest kab/kota or city, Halo Island asks Windows where the PC is, through Windows' own location service (Settings › Privacy & security › Location decides whether it may). It asks when the add-on starts and again every few hours, so a trip moves the place. The point is used on your PC to choose the nearest place, then dropped: it isn't saved and Halo Island sends it nowhere. Kemenag's kab/kota are in the add-on; cities outside Indonesia are looked up online, a small file at a time, from `kolijijani.github.io/halo-island/places/` (the website, hosted by GitHub Pages). Away from Kemenag's places, the location asks for the cities of the 5-degree squares (about 550 km across) around it; typing a place asks for the cities whose names start with the first two letters typed. Those requests carry nothing about you beyond what any page view carries (your IP address, and a User-Agent naming Halo Island and its version), and we get no record of them. Only the chosen place is saved, in your settings: its name, point and clock. Picking a place by hand stops the asking.

**Stocks, only while that add-on is on and its tab is open.** The Stocks tab shows the FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI and the stocks on your list with prices from [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com) (`query1.finance.yahoo.com`, no account and no key). While the tab is open, and never otherwise (not while the PC is locked or the screen is off), Halo Island asks Yahoo Finance for those prices: when the tab opens, then every minute while one of your stocks' markets trades and every 10 minutes while they're all shut. The request carries the symbols on your list and nothing about you beyond what any page view carries (your IP address, and a User-Agent naming Halo Island and its version); it sends and keeps no cookies. Bursa Malaysia's biggest companies are built in for the search; words you type that they don't match go to Yahoo Finance's search once you stop typing, and picking 1M, 6M or 1Y downloads that stock's daily closes for the year, once while the app runs. Your list is saved with your tasks; the prices are kept in memory only. Switch Stocks off in Add-ons to stop.

**Downloader, only while a download runs.** The Downloader add-on (on Windows and the Mac) saves the video or sound of a link you paste, with yt-dlp, a program that runs on your PC only while a download does. yt-dlp asks the site of that link for its page and downloads the video or sound from wherever that site keeps it. These requests go straight from your PC to the site, as your browser's would: the site sees your IP address, and it has its own terms. They carry none of your browser's cookies or sign-ins, and nothing about the link or the download goes to us or anyone else. Finished files go to your Downloads folder and onto the Shelf; the list of downloads is kept in memory only. Switching the Downloader off deletes yt-dlp and its other tools; the files you downloaded stay.

**IELTS, only while that add-on is on and you practise.** The IELTS tab is a speaking coach: Halo asks IELTS Speaking questions out loud and listens to your answers. It needs your Halo sign-in, and nothing happens until you press **Mulai**. While a session runs, the microphone records only between your taps on the mic (or until the question's time is up, or while Part 2's minute to prepare ends and the answer starts), into a WAV file on your PC, which is then sent with your Halo sign-in to Halo's server (`api.halo.app`). The server has Google's Gemini listen to it, to write Halo's next question and your scores, and ElevenLabs say Halo's lines; it keeps your level and best band with your Halo account. On your PC, the answer file is deleted once the server has it, and each of Halo's voice clips once it has played; leaving the session, switching IELTS off or signing out deletes whatever is left. Windows' own microphone switch (Settings › Privacy & security › Microphone) decides whether Halo Island may record at all; on a Mac, macOS asks you the first time, and System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone keeps your answer. Switch IELTS off in Add-ons to stop.

**Meeting notes, only while that add-on is on and you take notes.** The Notes tab writes up meetings. It needs your Halo sign-in, and nothing is recorded until you press the record button; it stops when you press **Stop**, or at 3 hours. While it records, it takes your microphone and your PC's own sound (what plays through your speakers or headset, so the other people in a call), mixed together, into files on your PC. On a Mac the Mac's own sound comes through a Core Audio tap of every app but Halo Island (macOS 14.2 and later), which macOS asks you to allow the first time; on macOS 14.0 and 14.1 only the microphone is recorded. After **Stop**, the recording is sent with your Halo sign-in to Halo's server (`api.halo.app`), which has Google's Gemini hear it and write the notes and the transcript (Google gets the recording for as long as that takes, and it's deleted from Google straight after). The server deletes the recording once it's heard, and keeps the notes only until your PC has fetched them, at most a day; it never adds them to your Halo account. On your PC, the recording is deleted once the notes are saved in `Documents\Halo Island\Notes\`, which are yours to keep, share or delete. If the notes can't be written, the recording stays on your PC for **Try again** until you throw it away; switching the add-on off deletes it. Windows' own microphone switch (Settings › Privacy & security › Microphone) decides whether Halo Island may use the microphone at all; on a Mac, macOS asks about the microphone, the sound of other apps and the Documents folder the first time, and System Settings › Privacy & Security keeps your answers. Recording a conversation can need the other people's agreement where you are: let them know you're taking notes. Switch Meeting notes off in Add-ons to stop.

Nothing else is sent anywhere. Music, volume, Bluetooth battery levels, the date and calendar, and the system stats are all read from Windows on your PC. The Caffeine add-on sends nothing at all: the drinks you add, its settings and its reminder are worked out and kept on your PC.

**Show over games reads Windows' frame log, only while it's on and a game is in front.** To count frames per second, Halo Island reads Windows' own log of the frames apps hand to the screen (event tracing, as PresentMon and Xbox Game Bar do) and keeps only the full-screen game's: when each frame came and which window it was for, the last two seconds of them, in memory. Nothing is loaded into the game, nothing is saved and nothing is sent anywhere. The one-time OK adds your Windows account to Windows' Performance Log Users group, which lets programs you run read Windows' performance logs; remove your account from that group to undo it (see the README).

**The Glass theme reads the screen behind the island, only while it's on.** To bend what's under the island, Halo Island copies the part of your screen behind its window (the island and the space it opens into) whenever something there changes. It doesn't copy while the island is hidden, while a full-screen app is in front, or while the PC is locked. The copy stays in your PC's memory and on its graphics card, and each new copy replaces the last. It is never saved, never shown anywhere else and never sent anywhere. While Glass is on, Windows leaves Halo Island out of screenshots, screen recordings and screen sharing. Switch to Classic or Halo to stop both.

**Notifications, only in the Microsoft Store copy, and only once you allow it in Windows.** In the Microsoft Store copy, Halo Island can read the notifications other apps show (the app's name and the notification's text). It's on from the start, but nothing is read until you press **Allow** on its tab and say yes to Windows' question. Then it reads each new one as it arrives, shows the ones from the apps you picked on the island for a few seconds, and sends nothing anywhere. Take it back any time in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Notifications, or switch Notifications off under **Tabs** in Settings. The download from GitHub can't read notifications and has no Notifications.

Uninstalling removes the app, its cache, its log, the add-ons' files, the Apple Music token, the paired phones, the AI tab's keys and the Halo sign-in (the key also stops working on Halo's side after 90 days unused, or at once if you disconnect it in halo.app). If you installed from the Microsoft Store, Windows also removes the files the app created. Data from an earlier copy installed from GitHub stays in `%APPDATA%\Halo\` until you delete it.

## Microsoft Store

If you install from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the download and updates under the [Microsoft Privacy Statement](https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement), and gives publishers summary reports such as install counts and crash reports.
